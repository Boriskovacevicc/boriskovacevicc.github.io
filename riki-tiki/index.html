How to play

Boki Riki Tiki is a family scorekeeper for Rikki-Tikki, a trick-taking card game. The first player to a trick leads; the aim is to win exactly as many tricks as you predicted — no more, no less.

Rounds

You choose the peak (max cards) on the setup screen. Rounds climb from 1 up to that peak, then back down to 1, without repeating the middle value. A peak of 1 is a single one-card round. The total number of rounds is always 2 × peak − 1.

  • 2–4 players: peak of up to 12 (23 rounds if you play the full length)
  • 5 players: peak of up to 10 (19 rounds)
  • 6 players: peak of up to 8 (15 rounds)

You can slide the peak down for a shorter match. With more people there aren’t enough cards for twelve each (one card is also turned up in the middle), so the maximum is capped automatically.

Dealing & bidding

The dealer moves around the table in order: player 1 deals round 1, player 2 deals round 2, and so on. Bidding starts with the player after the dealer, so the dealer always bids last.

Each player predicts how many tricks they will win, from 0 up to the number of cards that round.

Last-bidder rule: in rounds with more than one card, the final bidder may not choose a number that would make the bids add up exactly to the number of cards. That way it is impossible for everybody to be right. In the one-card rounds (the first and last of the match), this restriction is lifted, so everyone can be right.

Scoring

You can never score zero in a round. A perfect prediction always gains at least 10 points; a miss always loses something.

Hit your prediction: 10 + 2 × prediction

Miss: lose 2 × the difference

Predict 2 and collect 2 → +14

Predict 5 and collect 5 → +20

Predict 3 and collect 3 → +16

Predict 0 and collect 0 → +10

Predict 4 and collect 1 → −6

Predict 0 and collect 1 → −2

Using this scorekeeper

Enter bids before the round, then how many tricks each person actually collected. The app does the sums, keeps a running total, and remembers the game if you refresh the page. Tap a finished round in the scoreboard if you need to fix a mistake.

Finished matches are saved in History, and player names become profiles with wins and averages. You can export a backup file so the record isn’t lost if the iPad clears its storage.
